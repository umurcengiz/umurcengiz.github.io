Welcome!

I am a PhD candidate in Finance at KU Leuven and IÉSEG School of Management, supervised by Renaud Beaupain and Gunther Wuyts. I expect to defend my dissertation during the 2026/27 academic year and I am on the 2026–2027 academic job market.

I study how funding conditions and the scarcity of individual bonds as collateral shape liquidity and dealer quoting in sovereign bond and repo markets, and how central bank balance-sheet policies change these conditions. My work uses tick-level data from the MTS cash and repo markets for Italian government bonds.

Research interests: market microstructure, fixed income and repo markets, funding and market liquidity, central bank asset purchases

Working Papers

Special in Repo, Thin on the Ask [Job Market Paper] October 2026 Paper available on request
Abstract: We document how the two sides of the interdealer cash order book of Italian government bonds vary with repo specialness, using the best quotes, the full order book rebuilt from quote records, and executed orders. With bond and day fixed effects, a bond with 2 bp of specialness has 7.99 log points less depth at the best ask than a bond at the general collateral rate, while its quoted bid-ask spread does not differ. This difference holds within bond-months and with controls for the bond’s life cycle. Over longer horizons, special bonds also have more depth at the best bid and fewer executed sells relative to buys. Repo borrowing above its recent average does not reduce the best-ask depth of a bond at the general collateral rate. In our model of dealer quotes, a cost of sourcing a bond that is hard to borrow raises dealers’ reservation prices, and with discrete prices dealers then withdraw from the best ask and join the best bid.
Liquidity Push, Collateral Rush with Renaud Beaupain and Gunther Wuyts November 2026|Semi-finalist, Best Paper Award in Market Microstructure, FMA 2026 Paper available on request
Abstract: Central bank asset purchases and the refinancing operations that accompany them ease dealers’ funding by creating reserves, a liquidity push, but purchases also withdraw bonds from private portfolios and make them scarcer as repo collateral, a collateral rush. We study both effects in the cash and special-repo markets for Italian government bonds around the ECB’s Pandemic Emergency Purchase Programme (PEPP), at 15-minute intervals. During the programme, quoted bid-ask spreads in the cash market narrow by more than a third and top-of-the-book depth rises by about two-thirds, while specialness rises by about 70%. Intraday vector autoregressions show that cash-market shocks travel increasingly through depth, that specialness stops predicting repo volume and predicts repo volatility more strongly, and that transmission from the cash market to the repo market shifts towards depth.
Liquidity Spirals and Repo Specialness with Renaud Beaupain and Gunther Wuyts October 2026|Doctoral Consortium Award, SWFA 2026
Summary: We study how secured funding conditions and the liquidity of the cash and repo markets for Italian government bonds move together, using MTS data from September 2019 to February 2021 on a 15-minute grid. Funding is measured in the dealers’ own general collateral repo market, by the general collateral rate relative to the ECB deposit facility rate and by its volatility. A three-state regime model separates a floor regime, in which ample reserves hold the general collateral rate at the deposit facility rate, from an elevated and a stress regime. State-dependent local projections test whether the pass-through of funding shocks into special repo rates and the links between cash-market liquidity and funding change with the regime.
